# Post-Mortem: Hallucination Incident — Krka RAG Demo, March 2024
**Incident date:** March 14, 2024
**Type:** Model quality / confidence failure (not infrastructure)
**Author:** Anja Štrukelj
**Date written:** March 18, 2024
**Distribution:** Internal only — do not share with Krka

---

## What Happened

During the Phase 1 milestone demo for Krka Regulatory Affairs (March 14, on-site, Novo Mesto), the system produced a factually incorrect and confidently stated answer in front of four Krka employees including the RA Director (Mojca Hrovat) and a senior regulatory scientist (name withheld).

The question was: *"What is the EMA's current guideline on stability testing shelf life for oral solid dosage forms?"*

The system retrieved two relevant chunks from ICH Q1A(R2) and one from an older EMA guideline (2003). It then generated an answer that correctly cited the ICH guideline but fabricated a specific shelf-life figure (stated "36 months as the standard minimum" — this is not in any retrieved document and is not accurate as a general rule). The answer was delivered with no hedging, no uncertainty marker, and cited "ICH Q1A(R2) Section 4.1" — a citation which appeared credible but did not actually support the fabricated figure.

Mojca Hrovat caught it. She said, and I am quoting from my notes: "That's not right, and more importantly it's not in that section. Where is that number coming from?" We had no good answer. The demo effectively ended there.

---

## How This Happened

**The retrieval was correct.** The top-k chunks retrieved were genuinely the most relevant documents in the corpus for that query. The problem was in generation, not retrieval.

**The chunk containing the actual relevant text did not include a specific figure.** ICH Q1A(R2) discusses stability study design in terms of general requirements and conditions, not a single numeric shelf-life floor. The question implied a specific number existed; the model, rather than saying the document doesn't contain a specific figure, synthesized a plausible-sounding one.

**The citation was fabricated at the sub-document level.** The system was instructed to cite source documents, which it did correctly. It was not instructed to cite specific sections — we had added section-level citation to the roadmap but not implemented it. The model added "Section 4.1" without verifying that the retrieved chunk came from that section. It did not.

**Our confidence threshold was too permissive.** The retrieval confidence score for this query was 0.74 — above our 0.72 threshold but low enough that in retrospect it should have triggered a more hedged response. We had not yet implemented the confidence-to-hedging mapping that was in the original spec.

---

## Root Causes

1. **No confidence-to-hedging mapping:** High-confidence retrieval → assertive answer; lower-confidence retrieval → hedged or refused. This was spec'd but not implemented. The demo ran on a partially complete system — a mistake.
2. **Section-level citation not implemented:** The model invented a section number because it was instructed to be specific about sources and had no ground truth to cite correctly. We gave it an incentive to confabulate.
3. **Demo on pre-production system:** We ran the demo on the system as it was, not on a representative subset of queries we had validated. We knew there were gaps; we hoped the demo questions would avoid them. A Krka regulatory scientist asking about ICH guidelines is exactly the power-user case we had not tested.

---

## Immediate Response

In the meeting: Anja acknowledged the error, apologized, and explained that the demo system was a pre-production build. This did not fully land — from Mojca's perspective, a system that makes up regulatory figures is a system she cannot use. Fair.

The remainder of the demo was cancelled. We spent 30 minutes in conversation about the failure mode and what we were doing about it. Anja committed to sending a written technical explanation within 48 hours.

The written explanation was sent March 16. It described the root cause, the specific implementation gaps, and a revised timeline for addressing them. Mojca responded that she would reserve judgment until the next milestone.

---

## Remediation

- Confidence-to-hedging mapping implemented: retrieval score < 0.76 → answer prefixed with explicit uncertainty language; < 0.72 → refusal
- Section-level citation removed from the model's instruction set; replaced with chunk-level citation only (i.e., the system names the document and quotes the relevant passage, rather than inferring section numbers)
- A "specific numeric claim" detection heuristic added: if the answer contains a numeric figure not present in any retrieved chunk, the answer is flagged before delivery. This is imperfect but catches the most dangerous case.
- Pre-demo validation protocol established: any client-facing demo must be preceded by a 30-question validation run on the demo system the day before, with Žiga and Anja reviewing outputs. Not Luka.

---

## Lessons

The lesson is not "don't run demos on pre-production systems." Sometimes you have to. The lesson is: know specifically what your system cannot do and do not let it encounter those cases in front of a client.

We knew the confidence-to-hedging layer was missing. We should have either implemented it or restricted the demo to queries we had explicitly validated. We did neither. That's a process failure, not a model failure.

The second lesson is about section-level citations. We added that instruction because Krka asked for it and it sounded good. We did not think through the failure mode when the model cannot verify the citation. Never instruct the model to be specific about information it cannot verify.
