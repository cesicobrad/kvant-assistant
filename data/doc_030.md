# Meeting note — Iskratel / Kvant
**Date:** 7 April 2025  
**Location:** Video call (Teams)  
**Subject:** Iskratel response to MLOps platform proposal  
**Kvant attendees:** Maša Novak, Luka Peterlin (BD)  
**Iskratel attendees:** Gregor Hribar (Head of R&D Engineering), Tadeja Murn (IT Project Manager)  
**Note author:** Maša  
**Written:** same day

---

## Background

Kvant submitted a phased MLOps platform proposal to Iskratel in late February 2025, following the scoping work done in Q4 2024. Total proposal value: **€87,000** across three phases. Phase 1 was designed as a standalone deliverable — a baseline MLOps infrastructure (experiment tracking, model registry, CI/CD for ML pipelines) — priced at **€32,000**.

This meeting was Iskratel's formal response to the proposal.

---

## What they said

Gregor opened by saying the proposal had circulated internally and the general reaction was positive on the technical direction. He acknowledged the problem framing was accurate — they have model versioning chaos, no consistent way to track experiments, and repeated instances of "which model is actually in production right now" being unanswered quickly. He said, verbatim: "We agree with the diagnosis."

The pushback came on the total figure.

Gregor said €87,000 for the full scope is "not realistic for this year's budget cycle." He was direct about it — not apologetic, just factual. IT budget is locked until October; a new line item of that size requires a procurement process that takes two to three months minimum. Tadeja confirmed this and added that anything over €50,000 requires sign-off from a VP level that is currently occupied with a separate infrastructure migration.

On Phase 1 specifically, the reaction was meaningfully different. Tadeja said €32,000 is "within the range we can move on without the extended process." She didn't commit, but the phrasing suggested internal authority exists. She asked two questions: (1) what the Phase 1 delivery timeline looks like, and (2) whether Phase 1 creates vendor dependency that makes it difficult to not proceed with Phase 2.

Gregor added a concern that wasn't in their written feedback: they want the system to support their existing stack (primarily Python, some internal tooling built on .NET) rather than requiring a Kvant-specific abstraction layer. He mentioned MLflow explicitly as something they have looked at internally and would be comfortable with.

---

## Kvant responses

Luka addressed the vendor lock-in question directly: Phase 1 is built on open tooling (MLflow for tracking, standard CI patterns), and we can document the handover in a way that makes it independently operable. We would not be embedding proprietary Kvant interfaces. Gregor seemed satisfied with this.

On timeline, Maša said 8–10 weeks is a realistic Phase 1 delivery window assuming a clean start. Tadeja asked if we could compress to 6 weeks. Maša said she would check internally and follow up — did not commit on the call.

We did not revisit the total proposal figure during the meeting. The implicit framing that emerged was: close Phase 1, defer Phase 2 and 3 to a later budget cycle. Neither side made that explicit.

## Internal notes — not for client

The meeting went reasonably well but the budget constraint is real and the risk of Phase 2 never materializing is also real. If we close Phase 1 at €32,000, we should price it and staff it as a standalone, not as a loss-leader for the rest. The original proposal was structured with Phase 1 margins squeezed on the assumption Phase 2 would follow. That assumption is now shakier.

Tadeja is the actual decision-maker for Phase 1 — Gregor sets technical direction but procurement moves through her. She was warmer than the written feedback suggested. Worth a short follow-up call with her before we send the revised SOW, framed as "just want to make sure we're addressing the right things."

The 6-week timeline question is a flag. Either she has internal pressure we don't know about, or it's a test of whether we'll commit to something unrealistic. Rok needs to weigh in before we respond. My instinct is that 8 weeks is the honest answer and we should hold it. Saying 6 weeks and delivering in 8 is worse than saying 8 weeks up front.

The €87,000 total is probably dead for this year. The way to preserve the relationship for Phase 2 is to deliver Phase 1 cleanly and stay visible in October. If the MLOps work goes well, the follow-on is a reasonable conversation; if we rush Phase 1 to close faster, we make Phase 2 harder.

One thing that came up obliquely that we didn't address: Gregor mentioned their "internal ML team" twice. I'm not sure if that's aspirational or if there are actually people there doing ML work we don't know about. Worth understanding before Phase 1 scoping — if they have internal capability, the system needs to support their workflow, not replace it.
