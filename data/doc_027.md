## Workstream scope

The engagement covers AI-assisted quality review for translated marketing materials — primarily product brochures, website copy, and campaign materials — across four initial markets: Germany, Poland, Croatia, and Russia.

The core workflow problem: Krka's internal translators (or external LSPs) produce first-pass translations; these go to local market teams for review; local teams request corrections without a consistent framework; central marketing spends time adjudicating. The intervention is a lightweight LLM-assisted review layer that flags terminological inconsistencies, brand voice deviations, and formatting errors before materials go to local review.

This is meaningfully different from the RAG project. That work involved regulated content with traceability requirements and was built accordingly. This workstream is not regulated content in the same sense — marketing materials for pharmaceuticals are subject to advertising codes and some jurisdictional review requirements, but the review system we are building is *assisting* human review, not replacing it. That framing should be maintained clearly in all documentation.

Agreed scope:
- Prompt-engineering and evaluation framework for translation QA (4 languages initially)
- Integration with their existing review workflow (likely SharePoint-based, TBC)
- Terminology reference base built from approved Krka marketing glossaries
- Human-in-the-loop review interface (lightweight, browser-based)

Out of scope for this contract: new translation, regulatory review, legal clearance, or any content touching product labeling or prescribing information. If Luka starts to push on those boundaries, flag it immediately — that is Phase 1 territory and has different compliance architecture.

## Key differences from earlier Krka workstreams

For team members carrying over from the RAG or document classification work:

- **Audience is different.** Luka's team cares about brand and speed. The compliance vocabulary that works with Petra's team will not land the same way here.
- **Stakes are lower, but visibility is higher.** A mistake in a regulatory document is a serious compliance event. A mistake in a marketing brochure is embarrassing and time-consuming. Different kind of consequence, but marketing errors are visible to external markets in a way that regulatory misses often aren't.
- **Move faster than we did in Phase 1.** Luka was explicit: he wants something usable within six weeks. The Phase 1 timeline was appropriate for that context; this warrants a lighter build and earlier iteration.
