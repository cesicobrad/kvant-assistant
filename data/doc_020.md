# Technical Note: Lessons from the Krka Regulatory Pilot
**Author:** Anja Štrukelj
**Date:** 2024-01-08
**Type:** Short internal note — lessons learned

---

Three months into the Krka Phase 1 deployment, a few things are worth writing down before they fade.

**Slovenian pharmaceutical text is hard.** The embedding model (`text-embedding-3-large`) handles general Slovenian well but regulatory and pharmacopoeial vocabulary — terms like "stabilnostne študije," "učinkovina," "pomožna snov" — retrieves inconsistently, especially when the query uses the English equivalent and the document uses Slovenian. We partially addressed this with a query expansion step (generate both languages before retrieval) but it added latency. There may be a Slovenian-optimized embedding model worth evaluating. Žiga looked briefly at the SloBERTa embeddings but they're not designed for RAG retrieval — this is still open.

**Regulatory scientists know their documents.** The hallucination incident in March was bad, but the more general lesson is that domain experts catch errors immediately. The typical enterprise knowledge worker accepts a plausible-sounding answer; a regulatory scientist with 15 years of EMA submissions experience does not. Calibrate confidence thresholds more aggressively for expert users. Our standard 0.72 threshold was designed for a general enterprise audience and was wrong for Krka.

**The metadata problem was underestimated.** Documents without clean metadata (date, status, authority, market) made the retrieval filtering almost useless for the first six weeks. Žiga's document classification project (the standalone proposal, February 2024) exists because of this. Build metadata extraction into the ingestion pipeline from day one, not as a retrofit.

**The golden dataset workshop was the most valuable day on the project.** Sitting with two Krka regulatory scientists for four hours and writing 80 questions together was worth more than any automated evaluation. They wrote questions we would never have thought of. They also told us which questions mattered and which were edge cases. This should be in every project plan, not optional.
