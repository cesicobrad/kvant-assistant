## Client context

Pipistrel is a Slovenian aerospace manufacturer headquartered in Ajdovščina, internationally recognized as a pioneer in light electric aircraft. Their product line spans ultralight training aircraft, electric planes, and experimental platforms — including the Velis Electro, the first type-certified electric aircraft in the world. They operate within some of the most demanding regulatory environments in the aviation industry, subject to both EASA (European Union Aviation Safety Agency) certification requirements and, for export markets, FAA oversight.

Pipistrel was acquired by Textron Aviation in 2022 and has since operated as a semi-autonomous subsidiary. Day-to-day operations remain largely Slovenian, but reporting structures, tooling decisions, and compliance posture are increasingly shaped by the parent company's expectations. This creates a dual-audience problem for their documentation team: materials need to satisfy Textron's internal standards *and* external airworthiness regulators.

Their technical documentation workload is substantial. Aircraft certification requires Maintenance Manuals, Flight Manuals, Instructions for Continued Airworthiness, and a long tail of supplementary documents — all of which must be versioned, audited, and in some cases translated. The documentation team is small relative to the output volume, and informal feedback from their side during the pre-sales process was clear: they are under real pressure and open to tooling changes, but risk-averse about anything that touches the compliance layer directly.

This engagement was initiated after a brief introduction at an industry event in late 2024, followed by two scoping calls in January and February 2025. Pipistrel was cautious and thorough in those conversations — appropriate for the sector — and the framing that landed was "evaluate before you commit." The 4-week scoping structure was proposed by Kvant and accepted without significant modification.

## Business goals

The primary goal of the scoping project is to produce a concrete, prioritized map of where LLMs could reduce friction in Pipistrel's technical documentation workflow — without introducing new compliance risk.

Secondary goals:
- Identify which documentation processes are candidates for AI assistance in the near term (12–18 months) versus those that require more foundational work first
- Assess their current tooling ecosystem (likely legacy authoring tools — DITA or similar component content management) and understand integration constraints
- Give Pipistrel's leadership a credible basis for a go / no-go decision on a Phase 2 engagement

We are **not** being asked to build anything during the scoping phase. Deliverables are a landscape assessment and a Phase 2 proposal if the project proceeds.

---

## Technical environment constraints

Aerospace documentation operates under strict change-control requirements. Any tooling introduced into the documentation workflow will need to demonstrate auditability — who changed what, when, and under what authority. This is non-negotiable.

Key regulatory context:
- **EASA Part-21** governs design organization approval; documentation produced under this framework must meet defined quality standards
- **EASA Part-M** covers continuing airworthiness; maintenance documentation is subject to recurring audit
- **FAA FAR Part 21** applies to US market certification — relevant for at least two of Pipistrel's current programs

Data handling is a live concern. Textron has group-level information security requirements that will constrain which external services can process technical content. The scoping phase should include a data classification exercise: which documentation types contain controlled or proprietary technical data, and which are generic enough that standard cloud LLM APIs would be acceptable.

Do not assume open-model or public API access. Plan for private deployment scenarios from the start.

## Engagement model

**Week 1–2:** Stakeholder interviews, current-state documentation workflow mapping, tooling audit  
**Week 3:** Analysis and landscape review — where do LLMs fit, where do they create risk  
**Week 4:** Draft deliverables, internal review, client presentation

**Scoping deliverables:**
1. Workflow assessment report (written, Kvant-authored)
2. LLM opportunity landscape (prioritized shortlist with rationale)
3. Phase 2 proposal (if client signals intent to continue)

The engagement is scoping only. If the relationship continues, a Phase 2 SOW will be negotiated separately. Do not scope Phase 2 work informally during conversations — refer anything that starts to feel like Phase 2 to the proposal process.

## Notes for the team

Pipistrel is a good client for a long-term relationship if the scoping goes well. They are technically credible, their problem is real, and the aerospace documentation space has very few players doing serious AI work — which means if we build a reference case here, it travels.

Be patient with the compliance concerns. They are not obstruction for its own sake; they reflect genuine regulatory liability. Answers that acknowledge the constraint and work within it will land better than answers that minimize it.
